# Mission: Agentic AI — A Practical Starter

## Why
You're coming back from parental leave into a world where "agentic AI" has become the dominant industry
buzzword, at work and in the general press. You're not a software person and don't want to become one —
you want to understand what the term actually means, spot it when it shows up in tools you use (at home
and as a financial accountant), and know enough to ask sharp questions and use these tools sensibly,
without wasting time on hype or getting burned by trusting an AI agent with something it shouldn't be
trusted with.

## Success looks like
- Explain, in your own words, what makes an AI system "agentic" as opposed to a regular chatbot or
  autocomplete — and why that distinction matters in practice
- Recognize and correctly use the core vocabulary (model, LLM, prompt, token, context window, agent,
  harness, sandbox, tool use, hallucination, guardrails) when reading an article, a vendor pitch, or a
  tool's settings screen
- Name 2-3 concrete, realistic ways agentic AI is being used in accounting/finance work today (e.g.
  invoice matching, month-end close, reconciliation) and judge whether a claim about such a tool is
  plausible or overhyped
- Name 2-3 concrete, realistic personal uses (e.g. planning, research, inbox/calendar triage)
- Know the basic risk pattern — AI agents can act, not just talk, and can be confidently wrong — and what
  "human in the loop" / guardrails mean as the practical defense against that

## Constraints
- Non-technical background; no interest in building or coding agents, only in understanding and using them
- Limited time (returning from parental leave) — this pass should be brief and practical, not exhaustive
- Explicitly wants a first brief starter now, with deeper follow-up sessions later on specific topics
  (e.g. a hands-on look at a real tool, or a deeper dive into accounting-specific AI agents)

## Out of scope
- Building, coding, or configuring agents (prompt engineering, APIs, frameworks)
- Model architecture / how LLMs are trained internally
- Exhaustive vendor/tool comparisons — revisit only if a specific tool becomes relevant at work
